Sharing and Feedback

Sharing and Feedback

After participating in the First Cloud AI Journey program, I had the opportunity to study cloud computing, become familiar with AWS services, and contribute to a team project.

The program emphasizes self-learning, self-discipline, and individual initiative. Instead of receiving detailed instructions for every step, participants are expected to search for documentation, research possible solutions, and take responsibility for their assigned work. When I encountered difficulties, I could still ask questions and receive support from the program coordinators or other team members.

This approach helped me understand that independent learning and proactive problem-solving are important skills in a professional working environment.

General Evaluation

1. Learning and Working Environment

The program provided an environment in which participants could independently study and experiment with the knowledge they acquired. Participants were able to select their own approach, search for relevant documentation, and develop solutions suitable for their team assignments.

During the internship, most of the learning process and task completion depended on each participant’s initiative. This gradually helped me develop independent working habits, improve time management, and take responsibility for my work results.

The environment also helped me recognize the difference between classroom learning and implementing a practical project. In a real project, complete instructions are not always available. Therefore, participants must analyze problems, experiment with different approaches, and adjust their solutions.

2. Support During the Program

The program did not provide detailed instructions for every task. Instead, participants were encouraged to research independently before requesting assistance. When I encountered a problem that I could not resolve, I could proactively ask questions and receive guidance or support.

This support model helped me avoid becoming completely dependent on instructors. Before asking a question, I needed to search the documentation, investigate the issue, and clearly identify what I did not understand.

Through this process, I learned how to:

  • Search for and read technical documentation independently.
  • Describe technical problems more clearly.
  • Attempt multiple solutions before requesting support.
  • Receive feedback and adjust my approach.
  • Take responsibility for my decisions and work results.

In my opinion, this approach is suitable for developing independent learning skills and the ability to adapt to practical work.

3. Relevance to My Academic Major

The program content was relevant to my Information Technology major, particularly in the following areas:

  • Cloud computing.
  • Web application development.
  • System deployment and operation.
  • Source code management.
  • Databases.
  • Security and access control.
  • Teamwork.
  • Technical documentation.

The knowledge acquired at university gave me a foundation in programming, databases, and software development. During the internship, I expanded this foundation by working with AWS, Terraform, GitHub, Hugo, and related tools.

The program also helped me understand that programming knowledge is only one part of a practical project. Developers must also consider system architecture, infrastructure, security, testing, cost management, and deployment documentation.

4. Learning and Skill Development Opportunities

The aspect I appreciated most was that the program encouraged participants to conduct research and gain practical experience instead of only receiving information passively.

During the internship, I improved the following skills:

  • Searching for and selecting relevant documentation.
  • Reading AWS technical documentation.
  • Using the AWS Management Console.
  • Deploying infrastructure with Terraform.
  • Managing source code with Git and GitHub.
  • Dividing and coordinating team responsibilities.
  • Troubleshooting deployment issues.
  • Writing worklogs and technical reports.
  • Developing Workshop content with Hugo.
  • Presenting content in Vietnamese and English.
  • Sharing technical knowledge with the community.

The Live Auction project allowed me to encounter more practical problems than isolated academic exercises. When one component changed, the team had to consider how that change affected other components and agree on a suitable solution.

5. Self-discipline and Independent Working Ability

The program places considerable emphasis on self-discipline. Each participant must monitor their progress, complete the learning content, and contribute to the team product.

At first, I found it difficult to decide where to begin because the project involved many unfamiliar technologies. Over time, I gradually developed the following working process:

  1. Identify the requirement that must be completed.
  2. Research the relevant documentation.
  3. Divide the task into smaller parts.
  4. Experiment with each part individually.
  5. Verify the result.
  6. Record errors and their solutions.
  7. Communicate with other members when necessary.
  8. Organize the completed work into report documentation.

Having to work proactively for most of the program helped me become more independent and less dependent on predefined instructions.

6. Teamwork

The Live Auction project was developed as a team assignment. Therefore, each member had to take responsibility for a portion of the work and collaborate to produce a complete system.

During the project, the team worked together to:

  • Discuss ideas and deployment approaches.
  • Divide research topics.
  • Develop the frontend, backend, and infrastructure.
  • Test system functions.
  • Prepare the Proposal.
  • Develop the Workshop documentation.
  • Share source code and documentation through GitHub.
  • Review and improve each other’s content.

Through this experience, I learned that clear task assignment and regular progress updates significantly affect the overall result. If one member completes their tasks late or if different sections are inconsistent, the remaining members may encounter difficulties during integration.

This experience also helped me better understand individual responsibility within a team product.

7. Exposure to Practical Work

The program helped me become familiar with a result-oriented approach to work. Participants were not only required to understand the theory but also to develop a product, deploy the system, verify its operation, and prepare supporting documentation.

Through the project, I experienced the following process:

Understand the requirements
          ↓
Research possible solutions
          ↓
Design the architecture
          ↓
Divide team responsibilities
          ↓
Develop the system
          ↓
Deploy the system on AWS
          ↓
Test the system
          ↓
Prepare the Workshop and report

Although I still lack extensive practical experience, this process helped me better understand how a project progresses from initial research to a completed product and its documentation.

What I Was Most Satisfied With

The aspect I was most satisfied with during the internship was the opportunity to independently study AWS services and apply them to a specific project.

Instead of studying theory only, I participated in building and deploying the Live Auction system, used Terraform to manage the infrastructure, and verified the deployed resources through the AWS Management Console.

I was also satisfied that I completed:

  • Worklogs covering the full internship period.
  • The project Proposal.
  • Technical articles shared with the AWS Study Group.
  • The system deployment Workshop.
  • A bilingual report in Vietnamese and English.
  • The team’s shared product.

These results helped me realize that I could complete a relatively large amount of work by dividing it into smaller tasks, resolving each task persistently, and proactively requesting assistance when necessary.

Areas the Program Could Improve

In my opinion, the program could provide several additional elements to better support participants while maintaining its emphasis on independent learning:

  • Provide a general roadmap for each stage of the program.
  • Clearly define the expected results for each week.
  • Organize short and regular progress reviews.
  • Include additional experience-sharing sessions with previous participants or teams.
  • Provide recommended reference materials for each topic.
  • Establish the required Proposal, Workshop, and report structures early.
  • Offer an introductory session on designing AWS architectures.
  • Organize discussions between teams to share common problems and solutions.
  • Clarify product evaluation criteria at the beginning of the program.
  • Hold a final session where teams can demonstrate their products and receive feedback.

These improvements could help new participants find direction more quickly during the initial stage without reducing the requirement for initiative and independent research.

Would I Recommend the Program to Other Students?

I would recommend the program to students who:

  • Want to learn about cloud computing and AWS.
  • Can study independently and search for documentation proactively.
  • Are willing to experiment and troubleshoot problems.
  • Want to improve their ability to work independently.
  • Have a strong sense of responsibility when working in a team.
  • Want exposure to the process of implementing a practical project.

However, participants should understand that the program requires considerable individual effort. Students who only wait for detailed instructions or predefined answers may find it difficult to maintain progress.

Suggestions and Expectations

I hope the program continues emphasizing individual initiative because it helps participants improve their independent learning and adaptability.

I would also suggest the following improvements:

  • Establish clearer project milestones.
  • Organize regular discussions between program coordinators and teams.
  • Provide short feedback after each important stage.
  • Offer more examples of effective technical documentation.
  • Organize a shared demonstration session where teams can learn from each other.
  • Allow participants who have completed a topic to share their knowledge with other teams.

If given the opportunity, I would like to participate in future AWS-related programs, particularly those focusing on serverless architecture, Infrastructure as Code, security, monitoring, and cost optimization.

Conclusion

The First Cloud AI Journey program helped me realize that independent learning, initiative, and responsibility are essential in a professional working environment.

Although the internship involved challenges because I had to learn many new technologies and independently handle most of my responsibilities, it also gave me the opportunity to develop independence, teamwork, and the ability to receive and complete practical assignments.

I appreciate the knowledge, experience, and support I received during the program. These experiences will provide a foundation for me to continue improving myself and pursuing further development in cloud computing.
